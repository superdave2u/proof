# CURIOSITY — Cobalt · ◉ · Discovery · cards 11–20

Territory energy: **Discovery.** Curiosity begins when you stop needing to know where you're going.

## 11/52 — THE OTHER LINE

- **Type**: Invitation • Discovery
- **Quest**:
  - Choose something unfamiliar from a menu, counter, or your own pantry.
  - Ask what is in it and check anything you need for your dietary needs.
  - Try it with attention. You are free to dislike it.
- **Proof of Life**: Keep the receipt and write one honest sentence of review.
- **Special Stretch**: **Second Notice:** If you want another bite, look for a texture or flavor you missed before deciding what you think.
- **Art direction**: a night-market stall lit like an aquarium, an unidentifiable dish steaming under a single bulb, language dissolving into steam above it.
- **Flavor**: "The unfamiliar is only a habit you haven't met yet."

## 12/52 — ASK THE SHELF

- **Type**: Invitation • Encounter
- **Quest**:
  - Ask a bookseller, librarian, or reading friend: "What do you love that I might overlook?"
  - Borrow, browse, or buy something they suggest.
  - Read a little while the recommendation is fresh. Let one unexpected detail catch your attention.
- **Proof of Life**: Keep the receipt and copy the first sentence by hand.
- **Special Stretch**: **Their Doorway:** Ask, "What first drew you to it?" Let that answer guide where you begin.
- **Art direction**: a midnight-blue bookshop, a ladder leaning against the shelves, one book floating open in mid-air, constellations strung between the spines.
- **Flavor**: "Every bookseller is an undiscovered country."

## 13/52 — THE BUS WINDOW LOTTERY

- **Type**: Invitation • Pilgrimage
- **Quest**:
  - Choose a few accessible transit routes with a known way home and a budget and return time that work for you.
  - Board the first suitable arrival. Get off at an unfamiliar public stop within those limits.
  - Let something you notice choose your next small exploration. Return without needing a remarkable find.
- **Proof of Life**: Keep the ticket and one artifact from the stop.
- **Special Stretch**: **An Ordinary Ending:** If the place seems unremarkable, spend a little time with it instead of chasing a better destination.
- **Art direction**: a bus interior at dusk washed in amber light, the window framing a glowing street sign in a language you don't read.
- **Flavor**: "Transit is a slot machine that pays out in places."

## 14/52 — THE MUSEUM OF ONE THING

- **Type**: Invitation • Pursuit
- **Quest**:
  - Visit a museum, gallery, public display, or online collection.
  - Choose one object that makes you curious. Stay with it as long as you like.
  - Find one detail you would like to understand.
- **Proof of Life**: Sketch it, badly, by hand — the sketch is the artifact.
- **Special Stretch**: **Before the Label:** Invent a possible story for the object before reading its description. Let the difference be interesting.
- **Art direction**: a dark gallery with one floating illuminated artifact, its shadow stretching across the floor like a second, larger exhibit.
- **Flavor**: "Ten thousand things ask for your eyes. Give them to one."

## 15/52 — THE MASTER'S HANDS

- **Type**: Invitation • Discovery
- **Quest**:
  - Visit a public demonstration or ask a willing craftsperson: "May I watch for a few minutes?"
  - Watch before asking how it works. Notice a movement repeated, a pause, or a correction.
  - When there is an appropriate opening, ask about that specific detail.
- **Proof of Life**: Photograph their hands mid-work, and write down one detail you would have missed at a glance.
- **Special Stretch**: **Let the Answer Move:** Watch again after their explanation and notice what you can now see.
- **Art direction**: a workshop in late light, sparks and clay-dust suspended like fireflies, enormous weathered hands shaping something delicate.
- **Flavor**: "Mastery is invisible until you stand close enough to feel the heat."

## 16/52 — THE TOURIST AT HOME

- **Type**: Invitation • Pilgrimage
- **Quest**:
  - Visit a nearby place people come to see, including a free landmark you usually pass.
  - Approach it as a newcomer: read the sign or look from a different angle.
  - Stay with a detail you had never registered instead of moving on because you have already been here.
- **Proof of Life**: Keep a ticket, postcard, or photograph from it.
- **Special Stretch**: **Unembarrassed Interest:** Allow yourself one tourist pleasure you usually dismiss, such as posing for a picture or reading the whole plaque.
- **Art direction**: a golden-hour lookout crowded with visitors taking the same photograph, one figure in the middle of them glowing faintly as if newly arrived.
- **Flavor**: "You have never been here either."

## 17/52 — FOLLOW THE STRANGER'S MAP — CANON

- **Type**: Invitation • Discovery
- **Quest**:
  - Set aside a free hour or a workable opening. Ask someone: "Where nearby would you send me to see something you love?"
  - If it fits your access, safety, and budget, follow their suggestion instead of your usual choice. Check practical details; leave reviews unread.
  - Find out what the place is like without demanding that their taste match yours.
- **Proof of Life**: Return with one artifact from the destination.
- **Special Stretch**: **Stay with the Difference:** If it is not your kind of place, spend a few comfortable minutes finding what might matter to someone else before leaving.
- **Art direction**: a woman standing at a nighttime crossroads while a stranger sketches directions onto the back of a receipt. One road seems to glow faintly.
- **Flavor**: _"Curiosity begins when you stop needing to know where you're going."_

## 18/52 — THE FOREIGN SHELF

- **Type**: Invitation • Creation
- **Quest**:
  - Choose an ingredient you have never prepared and can comfortably obtain.
  - Learn a safe way to prepare it from a recipe or someone familiar with it.
  - Make a small amount and taste it when ready.
- **Proof of Life**: Keep the label or empty package beside a photograph of the dish.
- **Special Stretch**: **Your Own Palate:** After tasting, make one adjustment you want, even if the first attempt was already respectable.
- **Art direction**: a kitchen counter arranged like an explorer's table, one mysterious fruit or root under a beam of light, an open atlas beside the cutting board.
- **Flavor**: "The supermarket is an atlas you are allowed to take home."

## 19/52 — WHAT MOST PEOPLE GET WRONG

- **Type**: Invitation • Discovery
- **Quest**:
  - Ask someone willing to talk about their work: "What do people often misunderstand about what you do?"
  - Listen without offering a parallel example from your own expertise.
  - Ask one follow-up about something you genuinely did not understand. Let them choose when to stop.
- **Proof of Life**: Write the one sentence you will repeat to someone else.
- **Special Stretch**: **Say the Unknown:** Try: "I don’t know what that means. Would you explain it?" Give yourself permission to be a beginner in the conversation.
- **Art direction**: a craftsman's stall lit like a theater stage, the tools on the wall rearranged into a constellation, the worker's shadow taller than the truth of them.
- **Flavor**: "Everyone is a deep well you walk past daily."

## 20/52 — THE MAP YOU DRAW

- **Type**: Invitation • Creation
- **Quest**:
  - Draw a rough map of an accessible place you know, from memory.
  - Visit it and compare what is there with what you drew.
  - Add what you overlooked without erasing your first version.
- **Proof of Life**: Keep the drawn map beside a photograph of the real place.
- **Special Stretch**: **The Unmapped Detail:** Follow one overlooked feature briefly, even if it does not fit the story you usually tell about this place.
- **Art direction**: a hand-drawn map spread across a kitchen table, its streets glowing softly, the real streets visible faintly beneath like a second exposure.
- **Flavor**: "Memory is not a camera. It is a painter with opinions."
